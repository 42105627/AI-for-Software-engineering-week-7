
## **Part 1: Theoretical Understanding (30%)**

### **Q1: Define algorithmic bias and provide two examples of how it manifests in AI systems.**

**Definition:**
Algorithmic bias occurs when an AI system produces systematically unfair outcomes for certain groups, often reflecting existing societal prejudices or imbalances in training data.

**Examples:**

1. **Hiring Algorithms:** Amazon’s AI recruiting tool penalized resumes with female-associated terms, resulting in fewer women being shortlisted.
2. **Facial Recognition Systems:** Many facial recognition models have higher error rates for darker-skinned individuals, leading to misidentification and potential wrongful arrests.

### **Q2: Explain the difference between transparency and explainability in AI. Why are both important?**

**Transparency:**
Transparency is about **how the AI system is built, its structure, and its decision-making process** being accessible and understandable to humans.

**Explainability:**
Explainability is about **clarifying why a specific AI decision was made**, typically at the individual prediction level.

**Importance:**

* Without **transparency**, users and regulators cannot assess whether the system is fair or trustworthy.
* Without **explainability**, it’s impossible to hold the AI accountable for its decisions or correct harmful outcomes.

---

### **Q3: How does GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation) impact AI development in the EU?**

GDPR enforces strict rules on AI and data usage:

* **Consent:** AI systems must obtain explicit consent before processing personal data.
* **Right to Explanation:** Individuals can request an explanation of decisions made by AI.
* **Data Minimization:** Only necessary data should be collected and processed.
* **Accountability:** Organizations must demonstrate compliance and can face heavy fines for violations.

GDPR forces AI developers to design systems that respect privacy, fairness, and user rights.
